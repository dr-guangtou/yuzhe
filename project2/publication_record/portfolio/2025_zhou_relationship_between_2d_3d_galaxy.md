## Short Summary

We discover that projected 2D outer stellar mass—what astronomers actually observe—is not just a practical proxy but a *superior* (or at least equally powerful) predictor of halo mass compared to idealized 3D measurements, achieving as low as 0.2 dex scatter and producing unbiased weak lensing signals—enabling robust, observationally feasible cluster selection without requiring unattainable 3D data.

**中文：** 我们发现，天文学家实际观测到的二维投影外围恒星质量不仅是一种实用的代理参量，更是晕质量的一种更优（或至少同等有效）的示踪量；其与晕质量之间的弥散可低至0.2 dex，并能产生无偏的弱引力透镜信号——从而在无需难以获取的三维数据的前提下，实现稳健且观测上切实可行的星系团选源。

## Detailed Summary

This study addresses a critical observational challenge in modern galaxy–halo connection research: bridging the gap between the intrinsically three-dimensional physical properties of galaxies and their inevitable two-dimensional projections on the sky. While recent work has revealed that stellar mass in the outer regions of massive galaxies—beyond the half-light radius—serves as an exceptionally tight proxy for host halo mass, such measurements rely on idealized 3D information inaccessible to observers. Our work fills a vital methodological gap by rigorously quantifying how faithfully projected, observationally feasible 2D stellar mass estimates replicate the halo-mass predictive power of their 3D counterparts—a question with direct implications for cluster finding, weak lensing calibration, and cosmological inference from galaxy surveys.

Leveraging the high-resolution TNG300 simulation from the IllustrisTNG suite, we constructed a clean, well-resolved sample of 1,842 central galaxies at redshift *z* = 0.4 with stellar masses exceeding 10<sup>11.2</sup> M<sub>⊙</sub>. For each galaxy, we computed spherically averaged 3D stellar mass profiles out to 2*R*<sub>e</sub>, as well as their realistic 2D surface brightness projections—including full line-of-sight integration and realistic point-spread function convolution—to emulate ground-based imaging. We then defined “outer stellar mass” as the stellar mass contained between 1–2*R*<sub>e</sub> in both 3D and 2D, and assessed their correlations with true halo mass using scatter, rank-order correlation (Spearman *ρ* > 0.85), and conditional probability distributions. Crucially, we also stacked weak gravitational lensing signals around galaxies selected by both definitions to test for projection-induced biases in observable mass tracers.

Our results reveal a striking and encouraging robustness of the outer stellar mass concept across dimensions: the 2D outer stellar mass selection achieves a remarkably low scatter of just 0.20 dex in log *M*<sub>halo</sub> at fixed stellar mass—the best performance observed across all tested apertures and definitions—and matches or even marginally surpasses the 3D counterpart (0.21–0.22 dex) in predictive fidelity. Perhaps most significantly, the stacked weak lensing profiles for 2D- and 3D-selected samples are statistically indistinguishable across all radial scales probed (0.1–10 Mpc/h), confirming that projection effects do not introduce systematic bias in the inferred halo masses or shear profiles. This demonstrates that the outer stellar mass is not merely a theoretical curiosity but an observationally viable, low-scatter, and physically grounded cluster-finding metric—one that leverages existing deep imaging data without requiring spectroscopic redshifts or complex deprojection modeling.

These findings represent a creative synthesis of simulation-driven insight and observational pragmatism, transforming a conceptual advance into an immediately deployable tool for next-generation surveys like Rubin LSST, Euclid, and Roman. By validating the 2D outer stellar mass as a high-fidelity halo mass proxy, our work unlocks new pathways for constructing pure, mass-calibrated cluster samples with unprecedented statistical power—enabling tighter constraints on σ<sub>8</sub>, Ω<sub>m</sub>, and modified gravity models through cluster counts and lensing. Moreover, it reframes galaxy structural parameters not as secondary diagnostics, but as primary cosmological probes—elevating wide-field imaging from morphology cataloging to precision astrophysical metrology.

### 中文版

本研究致力于解决现代星系—暗晕关联研究中一项关键的观测挑战：弥合星系固有的三维物理属性与其在天球上不可避免的二维投影之间的鸿沟。尽管近期研究表明，大质量星系外围区域（半光半径以外）的恒星质量是宿主暗晕质量极为精确的示踪体，但此类测量依赖于理想化的三维信息，而这类信息对观测者而言无法直接获取。本工作通过严格量化二维投影、观测可行的恒星质量估计量在多大程度上忠实地复现其三维对应量对暗晕质量的预测能力，填补了一项至关重要的方法论空白——该问题直接关系到星系团搜寻、弱引力透镜定标以及基于星系巡天的宇宙学推断。

我们利用IllustrisTNG模拟套件中的高分辨率TNG300模拟数据，在红移 *z* = 0.4 处构建了一个干净且充分分辨的中心星系样本，共包含1842个恒星质量高于10<sup>11.2</sup> M<sub>⊙</sub> 的星系。对每个星系，我们计算了其球对称平均的三维恒星质量分布剖面，范围延伸至2*R*<sub>e</sub>；同时，也生成了符合实际观测条件的二维表面亮度投影图像——包括完整的视线方向积分及真实的点扩散函数卷积，以模拟地基成像观测。随后，我们将“外围恒星质量”定义为三维与二维情形下均位于1–2*R*<sub>e</sub> 区域内的恒星质量，并通过散射（scatter）、秩相关系数（Spearman *ρ* > 0.85）以及条件概率分布，系统评估其与真实暗晕质量的相关性。尤为关键的是，我们还分别依据二维与三维定义选取星系样本，叠加其弱引力透镜信号，以检验投影效应是否会在可观测的质量示踪体中引入系统性偏差。

研究结果揭示了“外围恒星质量”这一概念在不同维度间惊人的、令人鼓舞的稳健性：基于二维外围恒星质量所选样本，在固定恒星质量条件下对log *M*<sub>halo</sub> 的散射仅为0.20 dex——这是所有测试孔径与定义中所获最佳性能，且其预测保真度与三维对应量（0.21–0.22 dex）相当甚至略优。或许最具意义的是，二维与三维选取样本的叠加弱透镜剖面在全部探测径向尺度（0.1–10 Mpc/h）上均在统计学意义上不可区分，从而确证投影效应并未在推断的暗晕质量或剪切剖面中引入系统性偏差。这表明，“外围恒星质量”绝非仅具理论趣味的概念，而是一种观测上切实可行、低散射、且具有坚实物理基础的星系团搜寻指标——它可充分利用现有深度成像数据，无需依赖光谱红移或复杂的去投影建模。

上述成果代表了模拟驱动洞见与观测实用主义的一次创造性融合，将一项概念性进展转化为可立即应用于Rubin LSST、Euclid和Roman等下一代巡天项目的实用工具。通过验证二维外围恒星质量作为高保真度暗晕质量示踪体的有效性，本工作开辟了构建纯净、质量定标的星系团样本的新路径，显著提升其统计精度——从而借助星系团计数与透镜效应，对σ<sub>8</sub>、Ω<sub>m</sub> 及修正引力模型施加更严格的约束。此外，该成果亦重新界定了星系结构参数的角色：它们不再仅是次级诊断量，而跃升为首要的宇宙学探针——推动宽视场成像从形态分类迈向精密天体物理计量学。
