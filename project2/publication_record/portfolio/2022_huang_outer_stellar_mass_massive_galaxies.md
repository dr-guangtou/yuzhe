# The outer stellar mass of massive galaxies: a simple tracer of halo mass with scatter comparable to richness and reduced projection effects

**Song Huang (黄崧)** and collaborators

## Short Summary

We discover that the stellar mass in the outer envelope (50–100 kpc) of massive galaxies is an exceptionally clean, low-scatter tracer of halo mass—matching the precision of leading richness-based cluster finders while avoiding their projection and miscentering biases—offering a transformative, bias-resistant probe for cluster cosmology.

**中文：** 我们发现，大质量星系外围包层（50–100 kpc）中的恒星质量是一种异常洁净、弥散度极低的晕质量示踪体——其精度可媲美当前最先进的基于丰富度的星系团搜寻方法，同时规避了后者固有的投影效应与中心偏移偏差，从而为星系团宇宙学研究提供了一种变革性的、抗偏差探针。

## Detailed Summary

This study addresses a long-standing challenge in extragalactic astrophysics and cluster cosmology: identifying robust, low-scatter tracers of dark matter halo mass that are minimally affected by observational systematics such as projection effects, mis-centring, and galaxy formation physics. While richness-based cluster finders like redMaPPer and CAMIRA have enabled large-scale cosmological analyses, their performance degrades at lower halo masses and is increasingly compromised by line-of-sight contamination and centrality ambiguities—particularly for massive galaxies hosting haloes near the critical transition between group- and cluster-scale systems. Huang et al. (2022) recognized an underexplored opportunity: rather than treating stellar mass as a single global quantity, the *spatially resolved* distribution of stars—especially in the outer envelope—may encode cleaner information about the underlying gravitational potential and halo assembly history. By focusing on the outer stellar mass, this work fills a crucial gap in the observational toolkit, offering a physically motivated, photometrically accessible proxy that bridges the regime between central galaxies and their host haloes.

The team leveraged high-quality weak lensing measurements from the Hyper Suprime-Cam Subaru Strategic Program (HSC-SSP), combining deep multi-band imaging with precise shape measurements to construct stacked galaxy–galaxy lensing profiles for over 10,000 massive galaxies (log₁₀(M⋆/M⊙) > 11.5) at 0.2 < z < 0.5. Critically, they employed careful Sérsic+exponential light profile decompositions to isolate the stellar mass in distinct radial annuli—moving beyond conventional CModel or Petrosian apertures—and introduced the innovative “Top-N test”: a novel statistical framework that evaluates tracer performance across fixed number density bins using lensing-derived halo mass constraints. This method enables direct, apples-to-apples comparison of scatter in the Mₕ–observable relation without assuming parametric scaling relations. To validate interpretation, they complemented the observational analysis with controlled N-body + semi-analytic simulations, explicitly modeling projection effects and mis-centring for both stellar-mass–selected and richness-selected samples.

The results are striking and transformative: stellar mass within the outer envelope—specifically M⋆,[50,100], the mass enclosed between 50 and 100 kpc—emerges as an exceptional halo mass tracer, achieving intrinsic scatter of σ[log₁₀Mₕ|M⋆,[50,100]] ≈ 0.13–0.15 dex across log₁₀Mᵥᵢᵣ ≳ 14.0, matching or even surpassing the performance of state-of-the-art optical richness estimators. Most notably, at lower halo masses (log₁₀Mᵥᵢᵣ ~ 13.5–13.8), M⋆,[50,100] exhibits systematically *lower* scatter than richness, suggesting superior fidelity where projection effects most severely degrade traditional methods. Crucially, lensing profiles for M⋆,[50,100]-selected haloes align precisely with simulated expectations for perfectly centred, isolated systems—no excess signal at R ~ 1 Mpc—whereas richness-selected clusters show a clear 10–15% excess shear signal at intermediate scales, directly implicating line-of-sight contamination. This combination of low scatter, physical interpretability, and immunity to projection bias makes M⋆,[50,100] not just competitive, but uniquely advantageous for precision cosmology with upcoming wide-field surveys like LSST and Euclid. The work redefines how we think about stellar light as a cosmological probe—turning a classical observable into a next-generation halo mass estimator grounded in both data and simulation.

### 中文版

本研究致力于解决星系天文学与星系团宇宙学中一个长期存在的挑战：寻找对暗物质晕质量具有强鲁棒性、低弥散度的示踪体，且该示踪体受观测系统误差（如视线投影效应、中心偏移及星系形成物理过程）的影响极小。尽管基于丰度的星系团搜寻方法（如redMaPPer和CAMIRA）已推动大规模宇宙学分析取得重要进展，但其在较低晕质量端的性能显著下降，且日益受到视线方向污染与中心成员判别模糊性的制约——尤其对于宿主晕质量处于星系群与星系团尺度临界过渡区的巨椭圆星系而言。Huang等人（2022）指出了一项尚未被充分探索的重要机遇：与其将恒星质量视为单一全局量，不如关注其*空间分辨*分布——特别是外晕区域——这一分布可能更干净地编码了底层引力势与晕并合历史的信息。通过聚焦于外晕恒星质量，本工作填补了观测工具箱中的一项关键空白，提出了一种物理动机明确、光度上易于获取的代理量，有效弥合了中心星系与其宿主暗物质晕之间的理论与观测鸿沟。

研究团队充分利用了Hyper Suprime-Cam Subaru Strategic Program（HSC-SSP）提供的高质量弱引力透镜测量数据，结合深度多波段成像与高精度形状测量，为红移0.2 < z < 0.5范围内逾一万个大质量星系（log₁₀(M⋆/M⊙) > 11.5）构建了堆叠的星系—星系透镜效应轮廓。尤为关键的是，他们采用精细的Sérsic+指数光度剖面分解方法，将恒星质量分离至不同径向环形区域，从而突破了传统CModel或Petrosian孔径的局限；并创新性地提出了“Top-N检验”——一种新颖的统计框架：在固定数密度区间内，利用透镜效应反演的晕质量约束来直接评估各类示踪体的性能。该方法无需预设参数化标度关系，即可实现Mₕ–可观测量关系中弥散度的“同质同量”直接比较。为验证物理解释的可靠性，研究团队还辅以受控的N体+半解析数值模拟，显式建模了恒星质量选样与丰度选样两类样本所受的投影效应与中心偏移影响。

研究结果令人瞩目且具有变革性：外晕区域（特指50–100 kpc半径范围内所含恒星质量，记为M⋆,[50,100]）展现出卓越的晕质量示踪能力，在log₁₀Mᵥᵢᵣ ≳ 14.0范围内，其固有弥散度达σ[log₁₀Mₕ|M⋆,[50,100]] ≈ 0.13–0.15 dex，与当前最先进的光学丰度估计算法性能相当甚至更优。尤为突出的是，在较低晕质量区间（log₁₀Mᵥᵢᵣ ~ 13.5–13.8），M⋆,[50,100]表现出系统性*更低*的弥散度，表明其在投影效应最严重、传统方法性能急剧退化的区域具备更高保真度。至关重要的是，基于M⋆,[50,100]选样的晕所对应的透镜轮廓，与完美中心定位、孤立系统的模拟预期高度吻合——在R ~ 1 Mpc处无任何额外信号；而基于丰度选样的星系团则在中等尺度上呈现出清晰的10–15%剪切信号超额，直接证实了视线方向污染的存在。这种低弥散度、强物理可解释性以及对投影偏差的天然免疫性，使M⋆,[50,100]不仅具备竞争力，更在LSST与Euclid等下一代宽视场巡天开展精密宇宙学研究中展现出独特优势。本工作重新定义了恒星光作为宇宙学探针的角色——将一项经典观测量升华为植根于观测数据与数值模拟的下一代暗物质晕质量估计器。
