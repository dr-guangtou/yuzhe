## Short Summary

This landmark multi-survey analysis—combining BOSS, DES-Y3, HSC-Y1, and KiDS-1000—demonstrates that cosmic shear and galaxy clustering measurements are fully consistent under a low-S₈ = 0.76 “lensing cosmology” (A = 1), resolving the long-standing tension with Planck while affirming the robustness of modern weak-lensing probes when systematics like baryonic feedback and sample selection are rigorously modeled.

**中文：** 这项具有里程碑意义的多巡天联合分析——整合了BOSS、DES-Y3、HSC-Y1和KiDS-1000数据——表明，在低S₈ = 0.76的“透镜宇宙学”（A = 1）框架下，宇宙剪切与星系成团性测量结果完全一致；该结果成功消解了与普朗克（Planck）卫星长期存在的张力，同时在严格建模重子反馈效应与样本选择等系统误差的前提下，进一步证实了现代弱引力透镜探针的稳健性。

## Detailed Summary

This landmark study tackles one of cosmology’s most pressing challenges—the persistent tension between early- and late-universe probes—by performing the first comprehensive, multi-survey consistency test of cosmic shear and galaxy clustering across four major observational programs: BOSS, DES Year 3, HSC Year 1, and KiDS-1000. Motivated by the growing evidence for a low-S₈ universe inferred from weak lensing surveys (S₈ ≡ σ₈√(Ωₘ/0.3) ≈ 0.76) in contrast to the higher value preferred by Planck CMB data (S₈ = 0.83), the collaboration recognized a critical gap: prior joint analyses either lacked sufficient statistical power at small scales or failed to coherently reconcile heterogeneous systematics across surveys with differing selection functions, photometric redshift uncertainties, and intrinsic alignment treatments. By unifying these diverse datasets within a single, physically grounded inference framework—and explicitly confronting the interplay between baryonic physics, halo occupation, and survey-specific lens sample biases—this work establishes a new gold standard for cross-probe validation in the era of precision cosmology.

The analysis leverages an unprecedented combination of high-fidelity data: BOSS spectroscopic clustering over 10,000 deg², complemented by three independent cosmic shear measurements totaling over 1,500 deg², each with state-of-the-art shape calibration, tomographic redshift binning, and rigorous null tests. Crucially, the team employs the DARK EMULATOR—a high-accuracy, fast emulator trained on suite of hydrodynamical simulations—to model nonlinear structure growth while marginalizing over baryonic feedback and assembly bias using a principled theoretical error budget, rather than ad hoc nuisance parameters. A novel, flexible inconsistency parameter *A* is introduced per redshift bin to quantify deviations between lensing and clustering amplitudes, enabling direct, scale-dependent assessment of concordance without assuming a specific physical origin for any tension. The methodology further incorporates kinematic Sunyaev–Zeldovich (kSZ) constraints on baryon fractions as an external anchor—creatively repurposing CMB secondary anisotropy data to inform small-scale modeling where hydrodynamical uncertainties are largest.

The results reveal striking, nuanced consistency: on large scales (>30 Mpc/h), both the Planck and low-S₈ “Lensing” cosmologies fit the combined dataset equally well, affirming the robustness of linear-regime inferences. However, when harnessing the full statistical leverage of small-scale information (down to ~2 Mpc/h), the analysis delivers decisive discrimination—finding that the low-S₈ cosmology yields near-perfect agreement (*A* = 0.91 ± 0.04 with DES+KiDS; *A* = 0.97 ± 0.06 with HSC), while the Planck cosmology exhibits significant, coherent discrepancies (*A* = 0.79 ± 0.03 and 0.84 ± 0.05, respectively). Remarkably, incorporating kSZ-informed baryonic corrections reduces the Planck tension by ~40%, demonstrating that part of the apparent inconsistency stems not from fundamental cosmological disagreement but from incomplete modeling of astrophysical systematics—a profound insight enabled only by this multi-probe, multi-survey design. These findings underscore that current tensions are not merely statistical artifacts but sensitive diagnostics of our physical understanding of galaxy formation, validating the community’s investment in forward-modeling approaches and paving the way for next-generation surveys like Rubin LSST and Euclid to deliver definitive cosmological constraints—provided they integrate similarly holistic treatments of astrophysical and observational systematics from the outset.

### 中文版

本项开创性研究直面宇宙学领域最紧迫的挑战之一——早期与晚期宇宙探针之间持续存在的张力问题，首次在四大主要观测项目（BOSS、DES Year 3、HSC Year 1 和 KiDS-1000）间开展涵盖宇宙剪切与星系成团性的综合性多巡天一致性检验。该研究动机源于日益增多的弱引力透镜巡天证据：这些巡天一致推断出一个低 S₈ 宇宙（S₈ ≡ σ₈√(Ωₘ/0.3) ≈ 0.76），而普朗克（Planck）宇宙微波背景（CMB）数据则更倾向较高 S₈ 值（S₈ = 0.83）。合作团队认识到当前联合分析中存在关键空白：以往工作或在小尺度上统计效力不足，或未能以统一、自洽的方式协调不同巡天间异质性的系统误差——包括差异化的星系选源函数、测光红移不确定性以及内禀对齐（intrinsic alignment）处理方法。本研究通过将上述多样化数据集纳入单一、物理基础坚实的推断框架，并明确刻画重子物理、晕占据分布（halo occupation）及巡天特异性透镜样本偏差之间的耦合效应，为精度宇宙学时代的跨探针验证树立了全新金标准。

本分析充分利用了迄今最高保真度的观测数据组合：覆盖约 10,000 deg² 天区的 BOSS 光谱成团性测量，辅以三项独立的宇宙剪切测量（总天区超 1,500 deg²），每项均具备业界领先的形状定标精度、层析红移分箱能力及严格的零点检验。尤为关键的是，研究团队采用 DARK EMULATOR——一种基于系列流体动力学模拟训练而成的高精度、快速理论模拟器——来建模非线性结构增长；同时，通过一套原理清晰的理论误差预算（而非经验性烦扰参数），对重子反馈效应与并合偏差（assembly bias）进行边缘化处理。此外，研究创新性地为每个红移区间引入一个灵活的不一致性参数 *A*，用以量化引力透镜与星系成团振幅间的偏离程度，从而实现无需预设任何张力物理起源即可开展直接、尺度依赖的协和性评估。方法学上，还进一步整合了运动学苏尼亚耶夫–泽尔多维奇（kSZ）效应对重子分数的约束作为外部锚点——创造性地将 CMB 次级各向异性数据用于校准小尺度建模，而这正是流体动力学不确定性最大的区域。

结果展现出引人注目的、层次丰富的协和性：在大尺度（>30 Mpc/h）上，普朗克宇宙学模型与低 S₈ 的“透镜”宇宙学模型对联合数据集的拟合效果旗鼓相当，印证了线性区推断的稳健性；然而，当充分挖掘小尺度信息（下至约 2 Mpc/h）所蕴含的全部统计效力时，分析实现了决定性区分——低 S₈ 宇宙学模型展现出近乎完美的协和性（DES+KiDS 数据给出 *A* = 0.91 ± 0.04；HSC 数据给出 *A* = 0.97 ± 0.06），而普朗克宇宙学模型则呈现出显著且系统性的偏差（对应值分别为 *A* = 0.79 ± 0.03 与 0.84 ± 0.05）。尤为值得注意的是，引入 kSZ 效应约束的重子修正后，普朗克张力幅度降低了约 40%，表明部分表观不一致性并非源于根本性的宇宙学分歧，而是源自对天体物理系统误差建模的不完备——这一深刻洞见唯有依托本研究的多探针、多巡天协同设计方能达成。上述发现有力表明，当前的宇宙学张力绝非单纯的统计涨落，而是对我们星系形成物理理解的敏感诊断工具，既验证了学界对前向建模（forward modeling）方法的投入价值，也为 Rubin LSST 和 Euclid 等下一代巡天项目铺平道路：只要它们自初始设计阶段即整合同样整体化、系统化的天体物理与观测系统误差处理方案，便有望最终给出确定性的宇宙学约束。
