## Short Summary

This study reveals that in low-mass galaxies, high specific star formation rates drive Hα emission to become consistently compact and heterogeneous—evidence that dynamical instabilities concentrate molecular gas near the center—extending a key morphological–star-formation relationship to unexplored mass regimes for the first time using Merian Survey medium-band imaging.

**中文：** 本研究揭示，在低质量星系中，高比恒星形成率促使Hα辐射呈现出持续致密且不均匀的特征——这表明动力学不稳定性将分子气体聚集于星系中心区域；该成果首次利用梅里安巡天（Merian Survey）中波段成像，将这一关键的形态—恒星形成关系拓展至此前尚未探索的质量区间。

## Detailed Summary

This study addresses a critical gap in our understanding of star formation regulation in low-mass galaxies—specifically, how the spatial distribution of recent star formation (traced by Hα) relates to underlying stellar mass and dynamical state across the dwarf regime. While morphological analyses of star-forming galaxies have long focused on massive systems, the physical drivers of star formation in galaxies below 10¹⁰ M⊙ remain poorly constrained due to observational limitations: low surface brightness, small angular sizes, and contamination from nebular continuum and sky lines in narrowband imaging. The Merian Survey’s unprecedented combination of depth, resolution, and medium-band coverage—particularly its N708 filter centered on Hα at z ~ 0.09—provides the first opportunity to construct clean, high-fidelity Hα surface brightness maps for a statistically robust sample of bright dwarfs without relying on spectroscopic slit placement or model-dependent SED fitting. By targeting galaxies with 8 ≲ log(M⋆/M⊙) < 10.3 at 0.064 < z < 0.1, this work bridges the observational divide between local dwarf studies (where spatial resolution is high but cosmic variance and environmental effects dominate) and high-redshift surveys (where resolution and sensitivity limit morphological fidelity).

The team developed an innovative nonparametric framework that leverages the full seven-band Merian photometry—including the strategically placed N708 medium-band filter—to disentangle Hα emission from the underlying stellar continuum *without* assuming parametric galaxy profiles or requiring ancillary spectroscopy for each object. Crucially, they introduced a novel empirical method to estimate the stellar continuum directly within the N708 band using neighboring continuum filters (g, r, i), calibrated against available SDSS and MMT spectroscopy. This approach minimizes systematic bias from dust attenuation gradients and age–metallicity degeneracies that plague traditional broadband subtraction techniques. From the resulting Hα and continuum surface brightness maps, they computed robust nonparametric morphological diagnostics—including asymmetry (A), concentration (C), and Gini coefficient—for both components, enabling direct pixel-by-pixel comparison of star-forming and stellar light distributions across 127 galaxies. Their analysis further incorporated stellar masses, sSFRs, and gas fractions derived from integrated photometry and spectroscopy, allowing them to probe trends across physical parameter space with statistical rigor.

The results reveal compelling new insights into star formation physics in dwarfs: galaxies with the highest sSFRs (up to ~10⁻⁹ yr⁻¹) and lowest stellar masses (log(M⋆/M⊙) ≈ 8.2–8.8) exhibit Hα asymmetries (A_Hα ≈ 0.35–0.45) significantly higher than their continuum counterparts (A_cont ≈ 0.15–0.25), alongside compact, centrally concentrated Hα emission (C_Hα > 3.2) and elevated Gini coefficients (>0.6)—all signatures of intense, localized starburst activity. In contrast, quiescent dwarfs in the same mass range show diffuse, low-asymmetry Hα (A_Hα < 0.15) with morphology closely mirroring the older stellar population. Remarkably, the correlation between stellar continuum asymmetry and sSFR—previously established only for massive galaxies—holds robustly down to log(M⋆/M⊙) = 8.2, and now extends to Hα asymmetry itself. These findings strongly suggest that high sSFRs in dwarfs are not stochastic or environmentally triggered, but instead arise from internal dynamical instabilities—such as bar-driven inflows or turbulent gas compression—that funnel molecular gas into dense central regions, igniting compact, asymmetric starbursts. This work thus provides the first direct morphological evidence linking global galaxy dynamics to resolved star formation efficiency in the dwarf regime, offering a powerful new observational benchmark for hydrodynamical simulations and informing next-generation surveys like Rubin LSST and Roman.

### 中文版

本研究填补了我们对低质量星系中恒星形成调控机制认知的关键空白——具体聚焦于近期恒星形成（以Hα辐射示踪）的空间分布如何与底层恒星质量和动力学状态在矮星系区间内相关联。尽管对恒星形成星系的形态学分析长期集中于大质量系统，但质量低于10¹⁰ M⊙的星系中恒星形成的物理驱动机制仍受观测限制而难以约束：其面亮度低、角尺度小，且窄带成像易受电离气体连续谱及天光谱线污染。梅里安巡天（Merian Survey）凭借前所未有的深度、分辨率与中波段覆盖能力——尤其是其中心位于z ~ 0.09处Hα发射线的N708滤光片——首次为一批具有统计代表性的亮矮星系构建出干净、高保真度的Hα面亮度图，无需依赖光谱狭缝定位或模型依赖的SED拟合。本工作选取红移范围0.064 < z < 0.1、恒星质量范围8 ≲ log(M⋆/M⊙) < 10.3的星系，从而弥合了本地矮星系研究（空间分辨率高，但宇宙方差与环境效应占主导）与高红移巡天（分辨率与灵敏度限制形态保真度）之间的观测鸿沟。

研究团队开发了一种创新的非参数化框架，充分利用梅里安巡天全部七波段测光数据——特别是策略性布设的N708中波段滤光片——在不假设星系参数化轮廓、亦无需对每个目标开展辅助光谱观测的前提下，实现Hα发射与底层恒星连续谱的精确剥离。尤为关键的是，他们提出一种新颖的经验方法：利用邻近的连续谱滤光片（g、r、i）直接在N708波段内估计恒星连续谱，并以已有的SDSS与MMT光谱数据进行校准。该方法显著降低了传统宽带减法技术所固有的系统偏差，例如尘埃消光梯度及年龄–金属丰度简并性带来的影响。基于所得Hα与恒星连续谱面亮度图，研究团队计算了二者稳健的非参数形态学诊断量——包括不对称性（A）、集中度（C）及基尼系数（Gini coefficient），从而实现了对127个星系中恒星形成光与恒星光分布的逐像素直接比对。分析进一步整合了由积分测光与光谱推导出的恒星质量、比恒星形成率（sSFR）及气体分数，使研究得以在物理参数空间中以统计学严谨性探查演化趋势。

研究结果揭示了矮星系中恒星形成物理的新颖洞见：具有最高sSFR（达~10⁻⁹ yr⁻¹）与最低恒星质量（log(M⋆/M⊙) ≈ 8.2–8.8）的星系，其Hα不对称性（A_Hα ≈ 0.35–0.45）显著高于其恒星连续谱（A_cont ≈ 0.15–0.25），同时呈现紧凑且中心高度集中的Hα辐射（C_Hα > 3.2）与升高的基尼系数（>0.6）——这些均为剧烈、局域化星暴活动的典型特征。相比之下，同质量区间的宁静矮星系则表现出弥散、低不对称性（A_Hα < 0.15）的Hα辐射，其形态与年老恒星族高度一致。尤为引人注目的是，此前仅在大质量星系中确立的恒星连续谱不对称性与sSFR之间的相关性，如今稳健地延伸至log(M⋆/M⊙) = 8.2，并进一步扩展至Hα不对称性本身。这些发现强有力地表明：矮星系中的高sSFR并非随机过程或环境触发所致，而是源于内部动力学不稳定性——例如棒驱动的气体内流或湍流气体压缩——将分子气体输运至致密中心区域，从而点燃紧凑且不对称的星暴。因此，本工作首次提供了直接的形态学证据，将星系整体动力学与矮星系尺度上分辨的恒星形成效率关联起来，为流体动力学模拟树立了强有力的新观测基准，并为鲁宾LSST（Rubin LSST）与罗曼空间望远镜（Roman）等下一代巡天项目提供重要启示。
