## Short Summary

We discover that the stellar mass in the 50–100 kpc outskirts of massive galaxies is a uniquely powerful, simulation-validated proxy for dark matter halo mass—outperforming both total stellar mass and even the full ex situ (accreted) stellar mass—revealing a robust, observationally accessible tracer of halo assembly.

**中文：** 我们发现，大质量星系在50–100 kpc外围区域的恒星质量，是一种独特而强效的暗物质晕质量示踪体；该关系已通过数值模拟验证，其性能优于总恒星质量，甚至优于完整的外源（并合吸积）恒星质量，从而揭示出一种稳健且可观测的晕并合历史探针。

## Detailed Summary

This study addresses a critical and timely gap in our understanding of the galaxy–halo connection: how best to infer the mass of dark matter halos—fundamental tracers of cosmic structure growth—from observable stellar light. While total stellar mass and virial-scale proxies have long been used, they suffer from significant scatter and sensitivity to baryonic physics, especially in massive galaxies where feedback, mergers, and environmental effects complicate interpretations. Motivated by emerging observational evidence that extended stellar halos encode robust information about halo assembly history, we undertook the first systematic, simulation-based benchmarking of *spatially resolved* stellar mass as a halo mass proxy—specifically targeting the underexplored “outskirt” regime beyond 50 kpc, where stars are predominantly accreted and dynamically relaxed.

Leveraging the high-resolution cosmological hydrodynamical simulations Illustris, IllustrisTNG100, and IllustrisTNG300, we analyzed over 2,000 massive central galaxies (M<sub>⋆</sub> ≥ 10<sup>11.2</sup> M<sub>⊙</sub>) at z < 0.1. We computed stellar masses within multiple apertures—including standard 30-kpc and 100-kpc radii—as well as novel annular bins, with special focus on the 50–100 kpc region (M<sub>⋆,[50,100]</sub>). Crucially, we decomposed stellar populations into *in situ* and *ex situ* components using particle-tracking techniques, enabling direct comparison between spatially selected mass and physically defined accretion history. Our analysis incorporated rigorous statistical modeling—including scatter quantification, rank-order correlation tests (Spearman ρ), and residual variance decomposition—to isolate which metric delivers the tightest, most physically interpretable link to halo mass (M<sub>200c</sub>).

The results reveal a striking and robust finding: M<sub>⋆,[50,100]</sub> achieves a remarkably low scatter of just 0.11 dex in predicting halo mass across all three simulations—significantly tighter than total stellar mass (0.18 dex) or even the full ex situ stellar mass (0.14 dex). This outperforms not only conventional apertures but also challenges the intuitive assumption that *all* accreted stars equally trace halo mass; indeed, we find that only ~70% of the stars in the 50–100 kpc annulus are ex situ, yet this specific annulus yields the strongest correlation (ρ = 0.98) with M<sub>200c</sub>. Remarkably, despite stark differences in subgrid physics—particularly AGN feedback prescriptions—Illustris and IllustrisTNG100 converge on nearly identical ex situ fractions within this annulus, underscoring its resilience to model uncertainties. These findings establish M<sub>⋆,[50,100]</sub> as a uniquely powerful, observationally accessible halo mass proxy—one that is both empirically measurable with current wide-field imaging (e.g., HSC, DECaLS, LSST) and theoretically grounded in hierarchical assembly.

This work redefines how we bridge observations and theory in galaxy evolution. By identifying a simple, spatially localized stellar mass measurement that tightly correlates with halo mass—more so than even the full accreted stellar budget—it provides a new, low-scatter anchor for empirical scaling relations and a critical testbed for galaxy formation models. Its robustness across simulations with divergent baryonic physics suggests that the 50–100 kpc stellar annulus captures a universal phase in halo assembly: the settling of merger debris into dynamically cold, radially extended configurations that faithfully record the cumulative gravitational potential. For observers, it offers a path toward precision halo mass estimation without spectroscopic follow-up or dynamical modeling; for theorists, it reveals a diagnostic window into the physical fidelity of simulated stellar halos. Ultimately, this creative reframing of the “galaxy outskirts” as a calibrated cosmological probe opens transformative opportunities for leveraging next-generation surveys to constrain dark matter halo properties, test ΛCDM on galactic scales, and refine our understanding of how cosmic structure shapes galaxy growth.

### 中文版

本研究致力于解决星系—暗物质晕关联领域一个关键且亟待突破的科学问题：如何利用可观测的恒星辐射最有效地推断暗物质晕质量——这一表征宇宙结构增长的基本示踪量。尽管总恒星质量及基于维里尺度的代理量已被长期使用，但其存在显著弥散性，且对重子物理过程高度敏感，尤其在大质量星系中，反馈效应、并合事件及环境作用进一步加剧了物理解释的复杂性。受新兴观测证据的启发——即延展恒星晕蕴含关于晕组装历史的稳健信息——我们首次开展了基于数值模拟的系统性基准测试，将“空间分辨的恒星质量”作为暗物质晕质量代理量，并特别聚焦于尚未被充分探索的“外围区域”（半径大于50 kpc），该区域内恒星主要源于吸积过程且动力学已充分弛豫。

本研究充分利用高分辨率宇宙学流体动力学模拟Illustris、IllustrisTNG100与IllustrisTNG300，在红移z < 0.1处分析了逾2000个大质量中心星系（M<sub>⋆</sub> ≥ 10<sup>11.2</sup> M<sub>⊙</sub>）。我们计算了多个孔径内的恒星质量，包括标准的30 kpc与100 kpc半径孔径，以及创新性的环形质量区间，尤其重点关注50–100 kpc环形区域（记为M<sub>⋆,[50,100]</sub>）。尤为关键的是，我们采用粒子追踪技术将恒星成分分解为原位形成（*in situ*）与吸积形成（*ex situ*）两部分，从而实现空间选择的质量测量与物理定义的吸积历史之间的直接比对。我们的分析整合了严谨的统计建模方法——包括弥散度量化、秩相关检验（Spearman ρ）及残差方差分解——以甄别哪一指标能提供与晕质量（M<sub>200c</sub>）之间最紧密、物理意义最明确的关联。

结果揭示了一个显著且稳健的发现：在全部三项模拟中，M<sub>⋆,[50,100]</sub>预测晕质量的弥散度仅为0.11 dex，远低于总恒星质量（0.18 dex）乃至全部吸积恒星质量（0.14 dex）。该指标不仅优于传统孔径测量，更挑战了“所有吸积恒星均同等有效示踪晕质量”的直观假设；事实上，我们发现50–100 kpc环形区域内仅约70%的恒星属于吸积成分，然而正是这一特定环形区域展现出与M<sub>200c</sub>最强的相关性（ρ = 0.98）。值得注意的是，尽管Illustris与IllustrisTNG100在亚网格物理（尤其是活动星系核反馈模型）上存在显著差异，二者在该环形区域内的吸积恒星比例却高度一致，凸显了该区域对模型不确定性的强鲁棒性。上述成果确立了M<sub>⋆,[50,100]</sub>作为一种独特而强大的、可直接观测的晕质量代理量——它既可通过当前宽视场成像巡天（如HSC、DECaLS、LSST）实证测量，又在等级式结构组装理论框架下具有坚实基础。

本工作重新定义了星系演化研究中观测与理论之间的桥梁方式。通过识别出一种简单、空间局域化的恒星质量测量——其与晕质量的关联紧密度甚至超越整个吸积恒星总量——我们为经验性标度关系提供了新的低弥散锚点，并为星系形成模型构建了关键的检验平台。其在不同重子物理模型模拟中所展现的鲁棒性表明，50–100 kpc恒星环形区域捕捉到了晕组装过程中一个普适性阶段：并合遗迹沉降为动力学冷、径向延展的构型，从而忠实地记录了累积引力势。对观测者而言，该方法为无需光谱后随或动力学建模即可实现高精度晕质量估计开辟了新路径；对理论家而言，它揭示了一个诊断模拟恒星晕物理保真度的关键窗口。最终，这一创造性地将“星系外围”重构为经校准的宇宙学探针，为利用下一代巡天数据约束暗物质晕性质、在星系尺度上检验ΛCDM模型、以及深化我们对宇宙结构如何塑造星系生长的理解，开启了变革性机遇。
