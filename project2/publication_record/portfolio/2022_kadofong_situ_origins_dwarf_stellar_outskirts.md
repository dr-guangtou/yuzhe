## Short Summary

FIRE-2 simulations reveal that dwarf galaxies can build extended, round, old stellar halos *in situ*—not through mergers, but via outward migration of disk stars—a breakthrough that resolves the long-standing tension between observed halo ubiquity and insufficient merger histories in dwarfs. This discovery establishes stellar morphology as a powerful, observationally accessible diagnostic for testing galaxy formation physics, especially star formation feedback.

**中文：** FIRE-2 数值模拟揭示，矮星系可通过盘星族向外迁移（即原位形成）构建延展、近球对称且年老的恒星晕——而非依赖并合过程。这一突破性发现解决了长期存在的矛盾：观测上矮星系普遍存在恒星晕，但其并合历史却不足以支撑此类结构的形成。该发现确立了恒星形态学作为检验星系形成物理（尤其是恒星形成反馈机制）的一种强有力且易于开展观测验证的诊断工具。

## Detailed Summary

This study addresses a critical tension in near-field galaxy formation: the observed ubiquity of extended, old, and round stellar halos around high-mass dwarf galaxies—yet their apparent lack of sufficient minor merger history to build such structures via accretion. Prior theoretical work struggled to reproduce these halos without invoking implausibly frequent mergers or overly efficient stripping, leaving a significant gap in our understanding of how low-mass systems assemble their outer stellar components. By focusing on *in situ* origins rather than hierarchical assembly, Kado-Fong et al. reframe the problem entirely—asking not “where did these stars come from?” but “how do internal dynamical processes sculpt them into halo-like configurations?” This shift is both conceptually elegant and observationally grounded, directly confronting discrepancies between simulations and deep imaging surveys like Dragonfly and Subaru/HSC that reveal smooth, ancient outskirts even in isolated dwarfs.

Leveraging the FIRE-2 suite of cosmological zoom-in simulations—renowned for their physically motivated, high-resolution treatment of stellar feedback—the authors analyze a statistically robust sample of ~50 simulated dwarf galaxies spanning $10^{8.5} < M_\star/M_\odot < 10^{9.6}$ at $z=0$. They introduce a novel morphological diagnostic: the *intrinsic shape*, quantified via the 3D stellar mass tensor, which cleanly separates “disky” systems (with well-defined flattening and rotational support) from “nondisky” ones (triaxial, dispersion-dominated, or irregular). Crucially, they track stellar orbits and ages with full temporal resolution, enabling unambiguous identification of *in situ*-formed halo stars—not as remnants of disrupted satellites, but as disk-born stars dynamically heated and radially migrated outward over gigayear timescales by recurrent feedback-driven turbulence and bursty star formation. This approach moves beyond simple spatial cuts or kinematic proxies, grounding morphology in orbital histories and physical mechanisms.

The results are striking and definitive: disky dwarfs in FIRE-2 naturally develop thick disks *and* round, old ($\gtrsim 8$ Gyr), metal-poor ($\langle[\mathrm{Fe}/\mathrm{H}]\rangle \approx -1.4$) stellar halos—structurally and chemically consistent with observations—with up to $\sim 30\%$ of halo stars originating in situ via outward migration. In contrast, nondisky dwarfs—comprising nearly half the sample—exhibit chaotic, gas-poor, or burst-dominated evolution where feedback suppresses coherent disk formation altogether, yielding puffy, featureless systems that fail to match observed dwarfs. This bimodality is not an artifact of resolution or initial conditions but emerges directly from how feedback regulates gas cooling, angular momentum transport, and stellar kinematics. The work thus delivers the first self-consistent, high-fidelity demonstration that *internal* processes—not just external accretion—can forge canonical dwarf halos, while simultaneously establishing intrinsic shape as a powerful, quantitative benchmark for testing feedback physics in next-generation simulations. For observers, it provides testable predictions about age/metallicity gradients and kinematic lopsidedness in outskirts; for theorists, it offers a clear pathway to refine subgrid models by matching not just global scaling relations, but the *morphological diversity* encoded in stellar phase-space structure.

### 中文版

本研究聚焦近场星系形成领域一个关键矛盾：高质矮星系普遍拥有延展、古老且呈球状的恒星晕，然而其观测到的并合历史却似乎不足以通过吸积过程构建此类结构。此前的理论工作若要再现这些恒星晕，往往需引入极不合理的频繁并合事件或过度高效的潮汐剥离机制，从而在我们对低质量系统如何组装其外围恒星成分的理解中留下显著空白。Kado-Fong 等人摒弃层级式并合框架，转而聚焦于恒星的“原位”（*in situ*）起源，彻底重构了这一问题——不再追问“这些恒星源自何处？”，而是探究“内部动力学过程如何将它们塑造成类晕构型？”。这一范式转换既具概念上的简洁性，又具备坚实的观测基础，直接回应了数值模拟与 Dragonfly 和 Subaru/HSC 等深度成像巡天之间长期存在的分歧：后者在孤立矮星系中亦揭示出平滑、古老的外围区域。

作者依托 FIRE-2 宇宙学“缩放模拟”（cosmological zoom-in simulations）套件——该套件以物理驱动、高分辨率地刻画恒星反馈而著称——分析了一个统计上具有代表性的样本，包含约 50 个红移 $z=0$ 处的模拟矮星系，其恒星质量范围为 $10^{8.5} < M_\star/M_\odot < 10^{9.6}$。他们提出了一种新颖的形态学诊断方法：基于三维恒星质量张量定量表征的“本征形状”（*intrinsic shape*），可清晰区分“盘状”系统（具有明确的扁平化特征与旋转支撑）与“非盘状”系统（三轴、弥散主导或不规则）。尤为关键的是，他们以全时间分辨率追踪恒星轨道与年龄，从而明确识别出原位形成的晕星——这些恒星并非被瓦解卫星的残余，而是起源于盘内、并在数十亿年时间尺度上，经由反复发生的反馈驱动湍流与爆发式恒星形成所引发的动力学加热及径向迁移，最终抵达外围区域。该方法超越了简单的空间截断或运动学代理指标，将形态学特征牢固锚定于轨道演化历史与物理机制之上。

结果极为显著且结论明确：FIRE-2 中的盘状矮星系自然演化出厚盘，同时形成球状、古老（$\gtrsim 8$ Gyr）、金属丰度低（$\langle[\mathrm{Fe}/\mathrm{H}]\rangle \approx -1.4$）的恒星晕，其结构与化学性质均与观测高度一致；其中高达 $\sim 30\%$ 的晕星源于原位向外迁移。相比之下，样本中近半数的非盘状矮星系则呈现混沌、气体匮乏或爆发主导的演化路径，其反馈效应完全抑制了相干盘结构的形成，最终产生蓬松、无特征的系统，无法匹配实际观测到的矮星系。这种双峰分布并非分辨率或初始条件所致的人为假象，而是直接源于反馈对气体冷却、角动量输运及恒星运动学的调控方式。因此，本工作首次以自洽、高保真度的方式证实：**内部过程**（而不仅限于外部吸积）足以构筑典型矮星系晕；同时确立“本征形状”作为一项强大且定量化的基准，可用于检验下一代数值模拟中反馈物理的建模精度。对观测者而言，该工作提出了关于外围区域年龄/金属丰度梯度与运动学不对称性的可检验预言；对理论家而言，则提供了一条清晰路径——即通过匹配的不仅是全局标度关系，更是蕴含于恒星相空间结构中的**形态学多样性**，来优化亚网格物理模型。
