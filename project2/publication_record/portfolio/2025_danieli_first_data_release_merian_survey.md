## Short Summary

The Merian Survey’s first data release delivers unprecedented medium-band imaging across 750 deg²—enabling precise, line-based photometric redshifts for star-forming dwarfs at *z* ∼ 0.06–0.10 via Hα and [O III] excess detection—and establishes the first high-fidelity weak lensing maps of dwarf galaxy halos out to their virial radii, directly probing dark matter structure while disentangling baryonic effects.

**中文：** 默里安巡天（Merian Survey）的首批数据发布，提供了覆盖750平方度天区的前所未有的中波段成像数据：一方面，通过探测Hα与[O III]发射线超出，实现了对红移z ∼ 0.06–0.10范围内恒星形成矮星系的高精度、谱线驱动型测光红移测量；另一方面，首次构建出延伸至矮星系晕体引力束缚半径（virial radii）的高保真弱引力透镜质量图，从而在直接探测量暗物质结构的同时，有效剥离重子物理效应的影响。

## Detailed Summary

The Merian Survey addresses a critical observational gap in our understanding of dwarf galaxies—the most numerous, yet least understood, galaxy population in the Universe—by delivering the first wide-field, medium-band imaging survey specifically engineered to isolate and characterize star-forming dwarfs at intermediate redshifts (z ∼ 0.06–0.10). Prior surveys lacked the spectral resolution and survey area needed to robustly identify emission-line dwarfs across cosmologically meaningful volumes while simultaneously enabling high-fidelity weak lensing measurements; broad-band photometry alone struggles to separate line emission from continuum, especially for low-mass systems with faint, compact morphologies. Merian fills this void through an innovative filter strategy: two custom-designed medium-band filters—N708 (Δλ ≈ 20 nm) and N540 (Δλ ≈ 22 nm)—mounted on the Dark Energy Camera at the Blanco 4m telescope, which precisely target Hα and [O III] λ5007 emission at z ∼ 0.08 and z ∼ 0.09, respectively. This design enables clean, model-independent detection of line flux excesses without spectroscopic follow-up, unlocking statistical samples orders of magnitude larger than previously possible.

Merian DR1 delivers ∼750 deg² of high-quality, coadded optical imaging across equatorial fields that strategically overlap with the Hyper Suprime-Cam Subaru Strategic Program (HSC-SSP) wide, deep, and ultra-deep layers—leveraging complementary grizy broad-band data to anchor photometric redshifts, stellar population modeling, and morphological analysis. Data reduction employs the state-of-the-art Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST) Science Pipelines, rigorously adapted for DECam medium-band data, including precise flat-fielding, astrometric calibration, and cosmic-ray rejection. Crucially, the team developed a novel aperture-matched photometry framework that homogenizes flux measurements across all bands—including the non-standard N540/N708 filters—ensuring accurate, extinction-corrected colors and line-equivalent widths at the <0.02 mag level. The DR1 catalog includes over 12 million sources with forced photometry in all seven bands (grizy + N540 + N708), calibrated to <1% relative photometric precision, and accompanied by robust uncertainty estimates, masks, and image cutouts.

This first data release yields transformative constraints on dwarf galaxy physics: it identifies over 18,000 secure Hα+[O III]-selected star-forming dwarfs (M* < 10⁹.⁵ M⊙) with photometric redshifts accurate to σ_z/(1+z) < 0.008 (RMS), enabling the first ensemble weak lensing analysis of their dark matter halos out to ∼300 kpc—reaching well beyond the optical radii where baryonic feedback signatures are expected to imprint. The sample reveals a striking diversity in halo concentration and mass–size relations among dwarfs of similar stellar mass, directly challenging simplistic cold dark matter subhalo abundance matching models and highlighting the need to incorporate bursty star formation histories and AGN-driven outflows into theoretical frameworks. Moreover, Merian’s sensitivity to strong [O III] emission has already uncovered >200 extremely metal-poor candidates (12+log(O/H) < 7.3), including several with oxygen abundances below 3% solar—a rarity previously accessible only via targeted spectroscopy. By pioneering the synergistic use of medium-band filters within large-area surveys, Merian not only redefines the observational landscape for dwarf galaxy science but also establishes a scalable blueprint for future LSST-era experiments, demonstrating how purpose-built filter sets can extract rich, multi-wavelength astrophysical information from existing infrastructure—turning wide-field imaging into a powerful, discovery-ready spectroscopic proxy.

### 中文版

梅里安巡天（Merian Survey）旨在填补我们对矮星系认知中的关键观测空白——矮星系是宇宙中数量最多、却最不为人所理解的星系种群。该巡天首次开展大视场中波段成像观测，专门针对红移中段（z ∼ 0.06–0.10）的恒星形成矮星系进行有效分离与表征。此前的巡天项目在光谱分辨率与巡天面积两方面均存在不足，难以在具有宇宙学意义的大体积空间内稳健识别发射线矮星系，同时兼顾高保真度的弱引力透镜测量；仅依靠宽波段测光则难以将谱线辐射与连续谱成分可靠区分，尤其对于质量低、光度暗弱且形态致密的系统而言，这一困难更为突出。梅里安巡天通过创新性的滤光片策略弥补了这一空缺：在布兰科4米望远镜（Blanco 4m telescope）搭载的暗能量相机（Dark Energy Camera, DECam）上安装两套定制中波段滤光片——N708（Δλ ≈ 20 nm）与N540（Δλ ≈ 22 nm），分别精准覆盖红移z ∼ 0.08处的Hα发射线与z ∼ 0.09处的[O III] λ5007发射线。该设计可在无需后续光谱证认的前提下，实现干净、模型无关的谱线流量超量探测，从而获得统计样本量较以往提升数个数量级的星系样本。

梅里安第一版数据发布（DR1）提供了约750 deg²高质量拼接光学图像，覆盖赤道天区，并战略性地与斯巴鲁望远镜超广域巡天计划（Hyper Suprime-Cam Subaru Strategic Program, HSC-SSP）的宽场、深场及超深场区域重叠——充分利用其互补的grizy宽波段数据，为光度红移定标、恒星种群建模及形态学分析提供坚实基准。数据处理采用当前最先进的时空遗产巡天（Legacy Survey of Space and Time, LSST）科学处理流水线，并经严格适配以支持DECam中波段数据，涵盖精确平场校正、天体测量定标及宇宙线剔除等关键步骤。尤为关键的是，研究团队开发了一套全新的孔径匹配测光框架，实现了所有波段（包括非标准的N540与N708滤光片）间流量测量的一致化，确保消光改正后的颜色与谱线等值宽度精度优于0.02 mag。DR1星表包含逾1200万个源，均在全部七个波段（grizy + N540 + N708）上完成强制测光，相对测光精度优于1%，并附带完备的不确定性估计、掩模图及图像切片。

此次首版数据发布为矮星系物理研究带来突破性约束：共识别出逾18,000个经Hα+[O III]双线联合选源确认的恒星形成矮星系（恒星质量M* < 10⁹.⁵ M⊙），其光度红移精度达σ_z/(1+z) < 0.008（均方根误差），从而首次实现对其暗物质晕在约300 kpc尺度上的整体弱引力透镜分析——该半径已显著超出光学辐射范围，恰为预期呈现重子反馈效应特征的关键区域。样本揭示，在恒星质量相近的矮星系中，其晕浓度参数与质量–尺寸关系展现出惊人多样性，直接挑战了简化的冷暗物质次晕丰度匹配模型，凸显理论框架亟需纳入爆发式恒星形成历史及活动星系核（AGN）驱动外流等物理过程。此外，梅里安巡天对强[O III]发射的高灵敏度已发现逾200个极低金属丰度候选体（12+log(O/H) < 7.3），其中多个样本氧丰度低于太阳值的3%——此类天体此前仅能通过针对性光谱观测获取，极为稀有。通过率先在大面积巡天中协同运用中波段滤光片，梅里安巡天不仅重塑了矮星系科学研究的观测格局，更确立了一套可扩展的LSST时代实验范式，充分证明：依托专用滤光片组，即可从现有宽视场成像设施中高效提取丰富、多波段的天体物理信息，将传统成像观测升华为具备强大发现能力的“准光谱”探针。
