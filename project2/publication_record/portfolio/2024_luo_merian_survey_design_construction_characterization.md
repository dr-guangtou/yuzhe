## Short Summary

The Merian survey delivers the first high signal-to-noise weak lensing measurements around dwarf galaxies—achieving S/N ≈ 90 within 1 Mpc—by pioneering a custom-designed medium-band filter set (N540/N708) that enables precise photometric redshifts (σ<sub>Δz/(1+z)</sub> ≈ 0.01) for ~85,000 star-forming dwarfs, unlocking transformative tests of dark matter halo structure and stellar feedback in the low-mass regime.

**中文：** 梅里安巡天（Merian survey）首次实现了对矮星系周围弱引力透镜效应的高信噪比测量——在1 Mpc范围内信噪比达≈90；该成果得益于其首创的一套定制中波段滤光片（N540/N708），可为约8.5万个恒星形成矮星系提供高精度测光红移（σ<sub>Δz/(1+z)</sub> ≈ 0.01），从而为检验低质量区间的暗物质晕结构与恒星反馈机制开辟了变革性研究路径。

## Detailed Summary

The Merian survey addresses a critical observational gap in our understanding of galaxy formation at the faint end of the mass function: the lack of statistically robust, high-fidelity weak lensing measurements around dwarf galaxies. While cosmological simulations predict that dark matter halos hosting low-mass galaxies should exhibit distinct structural properties—shaped by baryonic feedback, reionization effects, and halo assembly history—empirical tests have been severely limited by small sample sizes, poor photometric redshift precision, and insufficient signal-to-noise in lensing shear measurements. Prior surveys either lacked the depth and resolution to resolve faint lenses or used broad-band filters ill-suited for cleanly isolating star-forming dwarfs at intermediate redshifts. Merian was conceived to break this impasse—not as a generic imaging survey, but as a purpose-built experiment that co-designs optical filters, observing strategy, and analysis pipelines to maximize sensitivity to both lens identification *and* lensing signal extraction simultaneously.

To achieve this, the collaboration designed and commissioned two custom medium-band filters—N540 (λ<sub>c</sub> = 5400 Å, Δλ = 210 Å) and N708 (λ<sub>c</sub> = 7080 Å, Δλ = 275 Å)—optimized through end-to-end simulations that jointly constrained photometric redshift accuracy, emission-line detection efficiency, and weak lensing S/N. These filters were installed on the 4-m Victor M. Blanco Telescope and deployed over ~60 nights using the Dark Energy Camera, strategically overlapping the Hyper Suprime-Cam Strategic Survey Program’s wide-field footprint to leverage existing deep HSC broadband data. The filter design is elegantly targeted: N540 captures [O III] λ5007 at z ≈ 0.08 and N708 captures Hα at z ≈ 0.08, enabling robust line-based redshift selection and star-formation classification for galaxies with stellar masses 10<sup>8</sup>–10<sup>9</sup> M<sub>⊙</sub>. Crucially, the bandpasses were tuned not only for astrophysical diagnostics but also to minimize systematic errors in shape measurement and photometric redshift estimation—embedding weak lensing systematics control directly into the hardware design.

The resulting survey delivers transformative capabilities: simulations confirm that Merian will yield a uniformly selected, spectroscopically validated sample of ~85,000 star-forming dwarf galaxies over 0.058 < z < 0.10, with exceptional photometric redshift performance—σ<sub>Δz/(1+z)</sub> ≈ 0.01 and an outlier fraction of just 2.8%. Most significantly, Merian achieves unprecedented weak lensing sensitivity: the stacked tangential shear profile around these dwarfs reaches S/N ≈ 32 within 0.5 Mpc and S/N ≈ 90 within 1.0 Mpc—orders of magnitude higher than any prior dwarf-galaxy lensing study. This enables precise inference of halo mass, concentration, and inner density profiles, allowing direct confrontation with predictions from hydrodynamical simulations that incorporate stellar feedback, supernova-driven outflows, and time-varying potential fluctuations. By bridging high-resolution imaging, emission-line diagnostics, and statistical weak lensing in a single, coherently designed framework, Merian establishes a new observational paradigm—one that turns dwarf galaxies from mere tracers into powerful probes of dark matter physics, baryonic regulation, and cosmic structure formation at the smallest scales.

### 中文版

梅里安巡天（Merian survey）旨在填补我们对星系形成认知中一个关键的观测空白——即矮星系质量函数暗端缺乏统计上可靠、高保真度的弱引力透镜测量。尽管宇宙学数值模拟预言，寄主低质量星系的暗物质晕应展现出由重子反馈、再电离效应及晕并合历史所塑造的独特结构特征，但相关经验检验却长期受限于样本量过小、测光红移精度不足，以及弱透镜剪切信号信噪比（S/N）偏低等问题。此前开展的巡天项目，或因深度与分辨率不足而无法分辨暗弱透镜源，或因采用宽波段滤光片而难以在中等红移处干净地分离出恒星形成活跃的矮星系。梅里安巡天正是为突破这一僵局而生：它并非一项通用型成像巡天，而是一项目标明确的实验性观测计划，其光学滤光片设计、观测策略与数据分析流程均经过协同优化，以同步最大化透镜星系识别能力与透镜信号提取灵敏度。

为实现上述目标，合作团队通过端到端模拟联合约束测光红移精度、发射线探测效率及弱透镜信噪比，专门设计并部署了两套中波段滤光片——N540（中心波长 λ<sub>c</sub> = 5400 Å，带宽 Δλ = 210 Å）与 N708（中心波长 λ<sub>c</sub> = 7080 Å，带宽 Δλ = 275 Å）。这两套滤光片已安装于4米维克多·M·布兰科望远镜（Victor M. Blanco Telescope），并利用暗能量相机（Dark Energy Camera）在约60个观测夜中完成拍摄；其观测区域战略性地覆盖了超广角主焦点相机战略巡天计划（Hyper Suprime-Cam Strategic Survey Program, HSC SSP）的宽视场区域，从而充分利用已有的深场HSC宽带数据。该滤光片设计精妙而富有针对性：N540可在 z ≈ 0.08 处捕获 [O III] λ5007 发射线，N708 则可在相同红移处捕获 Hα 发射线，从而为恒星质量处于 10<sup>8</sup>–10<sup>9</sup> M<sub>⊙</sub> 区间的星系提供稳健的谱线红移定标与恒星形成活动分类。尤为关键的是，这些波段不仅服务于天体物理诊断，更经过精细调谐，以最大限度抑制形状测量与测光红移估计中的系统误差——将弱透镜系统误差控制直接嵌入硬件设计之中。

由此产生的巡天数据具备变革性能力：模拟结果证实，梅里安巡天将在红移范围 0.058 < z < 0.10 内，提供一个均匀选取、光谱验证的约8.5万个恒星形成矮星系样本，其测光红移性能极为优异——σ<sub>Δz/(1+z)</sub> ≈ 0.01，异常值比例仅为2.8%。最具突破性的是，梅里安实现了前所未有的弱透镜灵敏度：围绕这批矮星系堆叠所得的切向剪切轮廓，在半径0.5 Mpc内信噪比达 ≈ 32，1.0 Mpc内更高达 ≈ 90——较以往任何矮星系透镜研究高出数个数量级。这使得对晕质量、浓度参数及内区密度分布的精确推断成为可能，从而可直接检验包含恒星反馈、超新星驱动外流及势能随时间变化涨落等物理过程的流体动力学模拟预言。通过在一个统一、协同设计的框架内整合高分辨率成像、发射线诊断与统计弱透镜分析，梅里安巡天确立了一种全新的观测范式——它将矮星系从单纯的示踪体，转变为探索暗物质物理、重子调控机制以及小尺度宇宙结构形成的强大探针。
