## Short Summary

This blind, cross-survey comparison of galaxy–galaxy lensing amplitudes—spanning six independent imaging surveys and over 100 authors—finds no evidence for large unknown systematics (<15% at 68% confidence), robustly ruling out lensing systematics as the sole explanation for the “lensing is low” tension at z < 0.54 and affirming that collaborative, empirical validation is a powerful path toward precision cosmic shear science.

**中文：** 这项针对星系-星系引力透镜效应振幅的盲态、跨巡天比对研究——涵盖六项独立成像巡天项目及百余位作者——未发现显著未知系统误差的证据（68%置信水平下小于15%），从而稳健地排除了透镜效应系统误差作为z < 0.54红移范围内“透镜信号偏低”张力唯一成因的可能性，并进一步证实：协作式、基于实证的验证是实现高精度宇宙剪切测量科学目标的一条有力途径。

## Detailed Summary

This landmark study addresses a critical and timely challenge in modern cosmology: the persistent tension between galaxy–galaxy lensing (GGL) measurements and predictions from galaxy clustering—commonly dubbed the “lensing is low” effect. While GGL has become a cornerstone probe of galaxy–halo connections and dark matter distribution, its interpretation hinges on precise control of systematic uncertainties in shear estimation, photometric redshifts, and blending effects—uncertainties that are notoriously difficult to quantify *a priori*. Prior efforts have largely assessed systematics within individual surveys, leaving open the possibility of shared, unmodeled biases or survey-specific artifacts masquerading as physical signals. The Lensing without Borders collaboration fills this gap with an unprecedented, blind, cross-survey validation framework—designed not only to test consistency but to *discover* where and how systematics may conspire across independent datasets, thereby transforming systematic error assessment from a theoretical exercise into an empirical discipline.

The analysis leverages a uniquely diverse ensemble of six high-quality imaging surveys—including HSC, DES, KiDS, CFHTLenS, RCS2, and CS82—each with distinct instrumentation, observing strategies, and data reduction pipelines, paired with a common set of BOSS spectroscopic lenses spanning 0.1 < z<sub>L</sub> < 0.7. Crucially, the comparison was conducted *blind*: lensing amplitudes (ΔΣ) were measured independently by each team using their own shear catalogs, photometric redshifts, and masking strategies, with no communication about results until all analyses were finalized and locked. Systematic error budgets were derived empirically—not from simulations alone—but through rigorous internal consistency tests, including jackknife resampling, null tests, and forward-modeling of known observational effects such as stellar contamination and blend-induced shear bias. This fully empirical, conservative methodology represents a creative departure from traditional top-down error modeling and enables robust, apples-to-apples comparisons across heterogeneous data.

The results reveal remarkable consistency across surveys at intermediate redshifts: for lenses at z<sub>L</sub> < 0.43, ΔΣ amplitudes agree to better than 2.3σ across four lens redshift bins and three radial ranges, validating current systematic error modeling in this regime. At higher redshifts (z<sub>L</sub> > 0.43), however, a statistically significant 3–4σ correlation emerges between lensing amplitude and survey depth—a subtle but telling signature pointing toward the growing influence of unrecognized galaxy blends and residual photometric redshift scatter. Even more intriguingly, above z<sub>L</sub> > 0.54, foreground stellar density begins to correlate with amplitude, suggesting that stellar contamination may be a previously underappreciated driver of high-z systematics. Critically, these trends remain comfortably within the empirically calibrated systematic error budgets—demonstrating that current frameworks are broadly adequate—but also enable stringent new constraints: systematic errors exceeding 15% (25%) are ruled out at 68% (95%) confidence for three key lens bins below z = 0.54. Moreover, the observed 20–30% discrepancies between lensing amplitudes and clustering-based predictions imply that lensing systematics alone cannot fully account for the “lensing is low” anomaly in this redshift range—pointing instead toward richer astrophysical or cosmological interpretations. By establishing cross-survey comparison as a powerful, scalable, and empirically grounded tool for systematic error discovery, this work sets a new standard for rigor in weak lensing science and paves the way for next-generation surveys like Rubin LSST and Euclid to achieve percent-level precision in galaxy–halo modeling.

### 中文版

本项具有里程碑意义的研究直面现代宇宙学中一个关键且紧迫的挑战：星系-星系引力透镜（GGL）观测结果与星系成团性理论预言之间持续存在的张力——这一现象被广泛称为“透镜信号偏低”效应。尽管GGL已成为刻画星系-暗晕关联及暗物质分布的核心探针，但其物理解读高度依赖于对剪切测量、测光红移及星系混叠效应等系统误差的精确控制；而这些误差恰恰以难以在先验层面准确量化的特性著称。以往工作多局限于单个巡天内部的系统误差评估，因而无法排除不同巡天间共有的、未建模偏差，或由巡天特有数据处理伪迹所伪装成的物理信号。Lensing without Borders合作组填补了这一空白，构建了一套前所未有的、盲态的跨巡天验证框架——该框架不仅旨在检验结果的一致性，更致力于主动“发现”系统误差如何在彼此独立的数据集中协同作用，从而将系统误差评估从一项理论推演真正转变为一门经验科学。

本分析充分利用了六组高质量成像巡天构成的独特多样化数据集——包括HSC、DES、KiDS、CFHTLenS、RCS2和CS82——它们在仪器配置、观测策略及数据处理流程上各具特色；同时统一采用BOSS光谱巡天提供的透镜星系样本（红移范围为0.1 < z<sub>L</sub> < 0.7）。尤为关键的是，整个比较过程严格实行“盲态”操作：各团队独立利用各自的剪切星表、测光红移及掩模策略测量透镜信号振幅（ΔΣ），且在全部分析完成并锁定结果之前，彼此不交流任何中间或最终结果。系统误差预算并非仅依赖模拟推导，而是通过严格的内部一致性检验经验性地确定，包括刀切法重采样（jackknife resampling）、零信号检验（null tests）以及对已知观测效应（如恒星污染与混叠导致的剪切偏差）的前向建模。这一完全基于经验、保守稳健的方法论，显著区别于传统自上而下的误差建模范式，从而实现了对异构数据集间真正“苹果对苹果”的可靠比对。

研究结果揭示，在中等红移区间内各巡天间呈现出惊人的高度一致性：对于z<sub>L</sub> < 0.43的透镜样本，在四个透镜红移区间及三个径向范围上，ΔΣ振幅的差异均优于2.3σ，有力验证了当前在此红移范围内的系统误差建模是可靠的。然而，在更高红移（z<sub>L</sub> > 0.43）处，透镜振幅与巡天深度之间却呈现出统计显著的3–4σ相关性——这一微妙但明确的特征，暗示着未识别的星系混叠效应及残余测光红移弥散的影响正逐渐增强。更引人深思的是，当z<sub>L</sub> > 0.54时，前景恒星密度开始与透镜振幅呈现相关性，表明恒星污染可能是一种此前被低估的、驱动高红移系统误差的关键因素。至关重要的是，上述所有趋势均稳稳落于经验标定的系统误差预算之内——这证明现有误差框架总体上是充分的；与此同时，该研究亦给出了迄今最严格的约束：在z = 0.54以下的三个关键透镜红移区间内，系统误差幅度超过15%（25%）的可能性，分别在68%（95%）置信水平下被排除。此外，观测到的透镜振幅与成团性预言之间20–30%的偏差表明，在此红移范围内，仅靠透镜系统误差尚不足以完全解释“透镜信号偏低”这一反常现象——暗示其根源更可能在于更丰富的天体物理过程或新宇宙学物理。本工作通过确立跨巡天比对作为一种强大、可扩展且经验根基坚实的新工具，用于系统误差的主动发现，为弱引力透镜科学研究树立了全新的严谨性标杆，并为Rubin LSST与Euclid等下一代巡天实现星系-暗晕建模的百分比级精度铺平了道路。
