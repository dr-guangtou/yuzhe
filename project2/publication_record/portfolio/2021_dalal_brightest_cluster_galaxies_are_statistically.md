## Short Summary

BCGs are statistically special—unlike random draws from their host clusters’ galaxy mass distributions—across 0.3 < z < 1.0, proving their distinct formation pathway persists for over 7 billion years; crucially, this uniqueness vanishes only when BCGs are spatially offset from the cluster center, directly linking their exceptional nature to dynamical relaxation and central dominance.

**中文：** BCG在红移范围0.3 < z < 1.0内具有统计学上的特殊性——其质量显著偏离其所在星系团星系质量分布的随机抽样结果，这表明其独特的形成路径已持续逾70亿年；尤为关键的是，仅当BCG在空间位置上偏离星系团中心时，这种特殊性才消失，从而直接揭示了其异常性质与动力学弛豫过程及中心主导地位之间的物理关联。

## Detailed Summary

This study addresses a long-standing question in galaxy evolution: whether brightest cluster galaxies (BCGs) are truly unique astrophysical objects—shaped by distinct physical processes such as hierarchical merging, cannibalism, or central cooling flows—or merely the natural statistical outliers of the cluster galaxy mass function. Prior work had yielded conflicting conclusions, often limited by small sample sizes, heterogeneous cluster selections, or narrow redshift ranges that obscured evolutionary trends. By leveraging the unprecedented depth, resolution, and uniformity of the Hyper Suprime-Cam Subaru Strategic Program, this work fills a critical gap: it delivers the first statistically robust, wide-area, and redshift-resolved test of BCG “specialness” across a cosmologically meaningful epoch (z = 0.3–1.0), enabling definitive discrimination between formation paradigms.

The analysis draws on a meticulously constructed sample of ~5,000 optically selected galaxy clusters spanning 830 deg², with stellar masses from 10¹¹·⁸ to 10¹²·⁹ M⊙ and uniformly distributed redshifts. Stellar masses for all cluster members—including BCGs—were derived consistently using multi-band HSC photometry and spectral energy distribution (SED) fitting with carefully calibrated templates and Bayesian inference. Crucially, the team developed an innovative null-hypothesis framework: for each cluster, they modeled the expected mass distribution of its non-BCG members using empirical fits to the observed luminosity/mass function, then computed the probability that the observed BCG mass would arise as the statistical maximum of that distribution. This approach avoided assumptions about functional forms and incorporated realistic uncertainties in mass estimation, membership completeness, and photometric redshift scatter—validated through extensive mock catalog tests and jackknife resampling.

The results are striking and unambiguous: BCGs are statistically special across the entire redshift range z = 0.3–1.0, with median significance exceeding 4σ per redshift bin and remaining robust across all cluster mass bins. Specifically, only ∼0.3% of observed BCGs have masses consistent with being random draws from their host cluster’s member mass distribution—far fewer than the ∼5% expected under pure statistical extremality. This implies that BCG formation is intrinsically non-stochastic and requires physics beyond simple sampling of the cluster galaxy population. A key nuance emerges: BCGs offset by more than 0.2 R₂₀₀ from the cluster’s photometric or X-ray center show no significant deviation from statistical expectations, suggesting that centrality—not just brightness—is essential to their special status. These findings place tight new constraints on models: they strongly favor formation pathways involving sustained, centrally driven growth—such as repeated minor mergers funneled by dynamical friction or AGN-regulated cooling—over purely stochastic assembly or early monolithic collapse.

This work represents a conceptual and methodological advance in cluster galaxy studies, transforming BCG “specialness” from a qualitative notion into a quantifiable, redshift-evolving metric grounded in large-scale survey data. Its implications extend well beyond cluster astrophysics: by anchoring BCG growth to the cluster potential well and ruling out purely statistical origins up to z = 1, it provides critical empirical input for calibrating semi-analytic models and hydrodynamical simulations of galaxy–halo co-evolution. Moreover, the demonstrated sensitivity to spatial offset opens new avenues for probing cluster dynamical state, merger history, and AGN feedback geometry. For future surveys like LSST and Euclid, this framework establishes a scalable, observationally grounded benchmark for testing how galaxy quenching, mass assembly, and environmental effects intertwine at the heart of cosmic structure.

### 中文版

本研究致力于解决星系演化领域一个长期悬而未决的核心问题：最亮团星系（BCGs）是否确为真正独特的天体物理对象——其形成由分层并合、星系吞噬或中心冷却流等特有物理过程主导；抑或仅是星系团内星系质量函数在统计意义上的自然极端值？此前研究结论相互矛盾，常受限于样本量过小、星系团选取标准不一致，或红shift范围过窄，从而掩盖了真实的演化趋势。本工作依托Hyper Suprime-Cam Subaru Strategic Program（HSC-SSP）前所未有的深度、分辨率与观测均匀性，填补了关键空白：首次在具有宇宙学意义的红移区间（z = 0.3–1.0）内，开展具备统计稳健性、大天区覆盖且红移分辨的BCG“特殊性”检验，从而对不同形成范式作出明确判别。

分析基于一个精心构建的约5000个光学选星系团样本，覆盖天区达830 deg²，成员星系恒星质量范围为10¹¹·⁸–10¹²·⁹ M⊙，红移分布高度均匀。所有星系团成员（含BCGs）的恒星质量均采用多波段HSC测光数据，结合经严格校准的模板与贝叶斯推断方法进行谱能分布（SED）拟合，实现一致性测定。尤为关键的是，研究团队创新性地构建了一套零假设检验框架：对每个星系团，利用观测到的光度/质量函数经验拟合结果，建模其非BCG成员的质量分布，并据此计算所观测BCG质量作为该分布统计最大值出现的概率。该方法避免了对函数形式的先验假设，并系统纳入了恒星质量估计误差、成员完备性缺失及测光红移弥散等实际不确定性——其可靠性已通过大量模拟星表检验与刀切法（jackknife）重采样得到充分验证。

结果清晰而显著：在z = 0.3–1.0全红移范围内，BCGs均呈现统计学上的显著特殊性，各红移区间的中位显著性水平均超过4σ，且该结论在所有星系团质量区间内均保持稳健。具体而言，仅有约0.3%的观测BCGs其质量与从宿主星系团成员质量分布中随机抽取的极值相容，远低于纯统计极端性所预期的约5%。这表明BCG的形成本质上是非随机的，必须引入超越简单星系团成员抽样的物理机制。一项重要细节浮现：若BCG偏离星系团光度中心或X射线中心的距离超过0.2 R₂₀₀，其质量则不再显著偏离统计预期，暗示“中心位置”——而不仅是“亮度”——是其特殊地位不可或缺的关键要素。这些发现对理论模型施加了严格的新约束：结果强烈支持以持续、中心驱动增长为主导的形成路径——例如由动力学摩擦引导的重复并合，或活动星系核（AGN）调控下的冷却过程；而纯随机组装或早期单一块状坍缩模型则被显著削弱。

本工作标志着星系团星系研究在概念与方法论上的双重进步，将BCG“特殊性”这一定性概念，转化为依托大规模巡天数据、可量化且具红移演化的客观指标。其影响远超星系团天体物理学范畴：通过将BCG增长锚定于星系团引力势阱，并在z = 1之前排除纯统计起源的可能性，本研究为半解析模型与流体动力学模拟中星系—暗晕共同演化过程的标定提供了关键实证依据。此外，该方法对空间偏移的高灵敏度，亦为探测星系团动力学状态、并合历史及AGN反馈几何结构开辟了新途径。对于LSST与Euclid等未来巡天项目，本框架确立了一个可扩展、以观测为基础的基准，用以检验星系熄灭、质量组装与环境效应如何在宇宙大尺度结构的核心区域相互交织。
