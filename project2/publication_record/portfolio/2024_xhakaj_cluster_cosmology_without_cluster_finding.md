## Short Summary

Supermassive galaxies from DESI deliver cosmological constraints on Ωₘ–σ₈ rivaling traditional cluster counts—but with access to lower-mass halos (down to ~10¹³ M⊙ h⁻¹) and significantly cleaner, more robust systematics than optical cluster finders. By leveraging mass-complete samples and projected clustering wₚ, this “cluster cosmology without cluster finding” opens a new, high-precision pathway to probe cosmic structure growth below the conventional cluster mass threshold.

**中文：** DESI巡天发现的超大质量星系所提供的Ωₘ–σ₈宇宙学约束，其精度可与传统星系团计数方法相媲美；但其探测范围可延伸至更低质量的暗晕（低至约10¹³ M⊙ h⁻¹），且系统误差显著更小、更稳健，优于光学星系团搜寻方法。该方法依托质量完备样本与投影关联函数wₚ，实现了“无需星系团识别的星系团宇宙学”，开辟了一条高精度探测量子尺度以下宇宙结构增长的新途径。

## Detailed Summary

This work pioneers a paradigm shift in large-scale structure cosmology by demonstrating that supermassive galaxies—selected cleanly and robustly via stellar mass completeness rather than traditional cluster-finding algorithms—can serve as powerful, high-fidelity cosmological probes on par with galaxy clusters. Motivated by persistent challenges in optical cluster cosmology—including systematic uncertainties tied to richness calibration, projection effects, selection function modeling, and the difficulty of reliably identifying low-mass halos—the study asks a fundamentally creative question: *What if we bypass cluster identification altogether and instead leverage the statistical clustering of the most massive galaxies as direct tracers of the underlying halo population?* By doing so, it fills a critical methodological gap: enabling cosmological inference from halo masses below $10^{13.5}\,M_\odot\,h^{-1}$, a regime largely inaccessible to conventional optical cluster surveys limited to richness $\lambda \gtrsim 20$, while simultaneously sidestepping many of the astrophysical systematics that plague cluster catalogs.

The analysis leverages realistic forecasts based on the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) survey, combining spectroscopic redshifts with stellar mass estimates to construct a fiducial mass-complete sample of supermassive galaxies ($M_* \gtrsim 10^{11.5}\,M_\odot$). Using halo occupation distribution (HOD) modeling calibrated to modern hydrodynamical simulations and validated against observational constraints, the team forecasts cosmological parameter constraints—particularly in the degenerate $\Omega_m$–$\sigma_8$ plane—from both number counts and projected two-point clustering ($w_p(r_p)$). Crucially, they compare performance across multiple sample definitions: a high-purity, mass-complete subsample; the larger but mass-incomplete DESI Bright Galaxy Survey (BGS); and conventional richness-selected clusters. All forecasts incorporate realistic survey geometry, photometric and spectroscopic incompleteness, and shot noise, while explicitly quantifying the impact of satellite galaxies—finding that even with a $\sim10\%$ satellite fraction, cosmological constraints remain robust under plausible modeling assumptions.

The results are striking and affirm the conceptual novelty of the approach: at matched number density, the mass-complete galaxy sample delivers $\Omega_m$–$\sigma_8$ constraints comparable in precision to those from richness-based clusters—yet it extends sensitivity down to halo masses of $\sim10^{12.5}\,M_\odot\,h^{-1}$, probing nearly an order of magnitude deeper into the halo mass function. Moreover, incorporating $w_p(r_p)$ significantly tightens $\Omega_m$ constraints beyond what number counts alone provide, highlighting the added value of spatial clustering information that is straightforward to measure for spectroscopic galaxy samples. Relative to the mass-complete benchmark, the BGS-like sample improves statistical precision by 60% for $\Omega_m$ and 40% for $\sigma_8$, underscoring the power of higher number densities—though the study wisely cautions that exploiting small-scale clustering in such incomplete samples introduces greater theoretical modeling uncertainty. Critically, initial systematics assessments reveal encouraging resilience: satellite contamination, assembly bias, and stellar mass estimation errors appear subdominant to statistical uncertainties in the forecasted parameter space. This work thus establishes supermassive galaxy cosmology not merely as a complementary technique, but as a strategically advantageous pathway toward lower-mass, higher-precision, and more robust cosmological inference—opening new avenues for DESI, Rubin LSST, and future spectroscopic surveys to deliver transformative constraints on dark energy and gravity.

### 中文版

本研究通过证明：利用恒星质量完备性（而非传统星系团搜寻算法）清晰、稳健地选取的超大质量星系，可作为与星系团媲美、高保真度的宇宙学探针，开创性地推动了大尺度结构宇宙学的研究范式转变。受光学星系团宇宙学长期面临的挑战所驱动——包括与丰富度定标相关的系统误差、投影效应、选择函数建模困难，以及低质量暗晕可靠识别的固有难度——该研究提出一个根本性的创新问题：“若我们完全绕过星系团识别步骤，转而直接利用最重星系的统计成团性作为底层暗晕总体的示踪体，结果会如何？”这一思路填补了一项关键的方法论空白：使宇宙学推断得以延伸至暗晕质量低至 $10^{13.5}\,M_\odot\,h^{-1}$ 的区间——该质量范围对受限于丰富度 $\lambda \gtrsim 20$ 的常规光学星系团巡天而言基本不可及；同时，该方法也规避了困扰星系团星表的诸多天体物理系统误差。

本分析基于暗能量光谱仪（DESI）巡天开展真实可行的理论预估，将光谱红移与恒星质量估计相结合，构建了一个基准的恒星质量完备样本（超大质量星系，$M_* \gtrsim 10^{11.5}\,M_\odot$）。借助经现代流体动力学模拟校准并由观测约束验证的晕占有分布（HOD）模型，研究团队预估了来自星系数密度和投影两点相关函数（$w_p(r_p)$）的宇宙学参数约束，尤其聚焦于 $\Omega_m$–$\sigma_8$ 的简并平面。尤为关键的是，研究在多种样本定义下对比了性能表现：高纯度、恒星质量完备的子样本；更大但恒星质量不完备的DESI亮星系巡天（BGS）样本；以及常规基于丰富度选取的星系团。所有预估均纳入了真实的巡天几何构型、测光与光谱不完备性以及散粒噪声，并显式量化了卫星星系的影响——结果表明，即便卫星星系占比达 $\sim10\%$，在合理的建模假设下，宇宙学约束仍保持稳健。

研究结果令人瞩目，有力印证了该方法的概念新颖性：在匹配数密度条件下，恒星质量完备星系样本给出的 $\Omega_m$–$\sigma_8$ 约束精度与基于丰富度的星系团相当，且其灵敏度进一步延伸至暗晕质量低至 $\sim10^{12.5}\,M_\odot\,h^{-1}$ 的区间，在暗晕质量函数中探测深度几乎提升一个数量级。此外，引入 $w_p(r_p)$ 显著收紧了 $\Omega_m$ 的约束，其提升幅度远超仅依赖星系数密度所能达到的效果，凸显了空间成团信息所具有的附加价值——而此类信息对于光谱星系样本而言易于精确测量。相较于恒星质量完备的基准样本，类BGS样本使 $\Omega_m$ 和 $\sigma_8$ 的统计精度分别提高60%和40%，充分彰显更高星系数密度的强大优势；不过研究亦审慎指出，在此类不完备样本中利用小尺度成团性将引入更大的理论建模不确定性。至关重要的是，初步系统误差评估展现出令人鼓舞的鲁棒性：卫星星系污染、组装偏差及恒星质量估计误差在预估的参数空间中均处于统计误差之下。因此，本工作不仅确立了超大质量星系宇宙学作为一种互补技术的地位，更将其提升为一条战略上更具优势的路径——通向更低暗晕质量、更高精度、更强鲁棒性的宇宙学推断，从而为DESI、Rubin LSST及未来光谱巡天开辟全新途径，以获取关于暗能量与引力本质的变革性约束。
