## Short Summary

BCGs defy typical galaxy scaling laws with a uniquely steep size–mass relation ($r_e \propto M_*^{0.73\text{–}1.00}$) that remains stable across cosmic time, while their dramatic size evolution—growing by 2.5× from $z \sim 1.2$ to $z \sim 0.2$—reveals minor mergers as the dominant driver of BCG growth, independent of host halo mass.

**中文：** BCG（最亮团星系）违背了典型的星系标度关系，其特有的陡峭的尺度–质量关系（$r_e \propto M_*^{0.73\text{–}1.00}$）在宇宙时标上保持稳定；而其显著的尺度演化——从 $z \sim 1.2$ 到 $z \sim 0.2$ 期间增大达2.5倍——表明并合过程以小质量并合为主导机制，驱动BCG增长，且该机制与宿主暗晕质量无关。

## Detailed Summary

This study addresses a critical gap in our understanding of galaxy evolution at the extreme high-mass end by delivering the first robust, observationally grounded size–mass relation for brightest cluster galaxies (BCGs) across an unprecedented redshift baseline of 0.1 < *z* < 1.4. While BCGs serve as unique probes of hierarchical assembly in dense environments, their structural evolution has remained poorly constrained beyond *z* ~ 0.5 due to limited deep, wide-field imaging and challenges in secure photometric identification. By leveraging the exceptional depth, resolution, and multi-band coverage of the Hyper Suprime-Cam Subaru Strategic Program (HSC-SSP), this work pioneers a homogeneous, mass-selected sample of 471 BCGs—spanning over an order of magnitude in stellar mass (log *M*<sub>*</sub>/M<sub>⊙</sub> = 11–12)—to trace how the most massive galaxies in the Universe have grown not just in mass, but in physical extent, over the past 9 billion years.

The analysis combines cutting-edge observational and modeling techniques: i-band effective radii (*r*<sub>e</sub>) are measured via high-fidelity Sérsic profile fits to HSC imaging, while stellar masses are derived from five-band (grizy) spectral energy distribution fitting applied to the same Sérsic-modelled photometry—ensuring self-consistent structural and stellar population measurements. Crucially, the team implements rigorous photometric selection criteria validated against spectroscopic follow-up and cluster catalogs, mitigating contamination and enabling robust statistical inference across cosmic time. This methodological coherence, paired with careful treatment of PSF effects, surface brightness limits, and mass completeness, establishes a new benchmark for extragalactic structural studies at intermediate redshifts.

The results reveal a strikingly steep size–mass scaling relation, *r*<sub>e</sub> ∝ *M*<sub>*</sub><sup>0.73–1.00</sup>, whose slope remains remarkably constant out to *z* ≈ 1—demonstrating that the structural hierarchy among BCGs is already in place early in cosmic history. More dramatically, BCGs exhibit rapid size evolution at fixed mass: *r*<sub>e</sub> ∝ (1 + *z*)<sup>−1.58 ± 0.13</sup>, growing by a factor of 2.5 between *z* ≈ 1.2 and *z* ≈ 0.2. This strong, monotonic expansion strongly favors frequent minor mergers over major mergers or in-situ star formation as the dominant growth channel—a conclusion elegantly corroborated by agreement with modern semi-analytical models. Furthermore, the absence of a significant correlation between BCG size and host halo mass suggests that environmental influence operates primarily on the outer stellar envelopes rather than setting the global structural scale, pointing to a decoupling between cluster-scale potential and BCG central structure.

These findings fundamentally reshape how we interpret BCGs—not merely as passive endpoints of evolution, but as dynamic laboratories where merger-driven accretion imprints clear, measurable signatures on galaxy morphology across cosmic time. The tight, non-evolving slope underscores their distinct evolutionary pathway relative to field galaxies, while the quantified size evolution provides a powerful new constraint for testing galaxy formation physics in cosmological simulations. For both theoretical modeling and future observational programs—including those with JWST and Rubin Observatory—the work delivers a definitive empirical framework for connecting BCG growth to the underlying dark matter halo assembly, making it an essential reference for advancing our understanding of mass assembly at the pinnacle of cosmic structure.

### 中文版

本研究通过构建迄今红移范围最广（0.1 < *z* < 1.4）的、基于观测的最亮团星系（BCGs）尺寸–质量关系，填补了我们对宇宙极端高质端星系演化认知中的关键空白。尽管BCGs作为致密环境中等级式并合的独特探针具有重要价值，但受限于深度宽视场成像数据的匮乏以及光度证认的困难，其结构演化在 *z* ≈ 0.5 以外一直缺乏可靠约束。本工作充分利用Hyper Suprime-Cam Subaru Strategic Program（HSC-SSP）巡天所具备的卓越深度、高空间分辨率及多波段覆盖优势，首次构建了一个均匀、质量选源的BCG样本，共包含471个目标，恒星质量跨越一个数量级以上（log *M*<sub>*</sub>/M<sub>⊙</sub> = 11–12），从而系统追踪宇宙中质量最大的星系在过去90亿年间不仅如何增质，更如何扩径。

分析融合了前沿的观测与建模技术：利用HSC成像数据，通过高保真Sérsic剖面拟合精确测定*i*波段有效半径（*r*<sub>e</sub>）；同时，基于同一Sérsic模型化的测光数据，开展五波段（grizy）能谱能量分布（SED）拟合以推导恒星质量——确保结构参数与恒星种群性质测量的高度自洽。尤为关键的是，研究团队制定了严格的光度选源标准，并经光谱后随观测及星系团星表交叉验证，显著抑制了污染，从而实现了跨越宇宙时间尺度的稳健统计推断。该方法学的一致性，辅以对点扩散函数（PSF）效应、面亮度极限及质量完备性的审慎处理，为中等红移区的河外星系结构研究树立了新基准。

结果揭示了一条异常陡峭的尺寸–质量标度关系：*r*<sub>e</sub> ∝ *M*<sub>*</sub><sup>0.73–1.00</sup>，其斜率在红移高达 *z* ≈ 1 的范围内保持惊人恒定——表明BCGs间的结构层级早在宇宙早期即已确立。更引人注目的是，BCGs在固定质量下展现出剧烈的尺寸演化：*r*<sub>e</sub> ∝ (1 + *z*)<sup>−1.58 ± 0.13</sup>，即在 *z* ≈ 1.2 至 *z* ≈ 0.2 期间，其有效半径增长达2.5倍。这一强烈且单调的扩张趋势明确支持频繁的并合（minor mergers）而非主并合（major mergers）或原位恒星形成（in-situ star formation）作为主导增长机制；该结论亦得到现代半解析模型的优美印证。此外，BCG尺寸与其宿主暗晕质量之间缺乏显著相关性，暗示环境影响主要作用于星系外围恒星包层，而非决定其整体结构尺度，反映出星系团尺度引力势与BCG中心结构之间存在解耦。

这些发现从根本上重塑了我们对BCGs的认知——它们不仅是演化的被动终点，更是动态的实验室，在其中由并合驱动的吸积过程于星系形态上留下了清晰可测的印记，并贯穿整个宇宙时间。该关系紧密且不随红移演化的斜率，凸显了BCGs区别于场星系的独特演化路径；而所量化的尺寸演化则为检验宇宙学模拟中的星系形成物理提供了强有力的新约束。对于理论建模及未来观测项目——包括詹姆斯·韦布空间望远镜（JWST）与鲁宾天文台（Rubin Observatory）的相关计划——本工作提供了一个确定性的经验框架，将BCG增长与底层暗晕组装紧密关联，因而成为深化我们对宇宙大尺度结构顶端质量组装理解不可或缺的参考依据。
