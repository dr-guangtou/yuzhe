# Roadmap for a More Automatic Research Workflow

## Project 1: Daily arXiv Summary

- Motivation: as more and more preprints hit arXiv every day, it is getting more and more difficult to follow the arXiv. I need a personal assistant who can help me filter the daily arXiv updates, identify the papers I am interested in, and provide quick summaries of the most relevant ones. I want a tool that automatically runs daily to check for updates, sends me a list of relevant papers, and generates detailed summary files. With enough time, I hope that this tool can also help me build a database of the recent arXiv preprints.
- Status: Under Testing
- Specification: `PROJECT1_SPEC.md`
- Folder: `project`

## Project 2: Personal Publication Record

- Motivation: I'd like to maintain a summary document for my own publications. I need these summaries for my own personal website and for grant applications. I want something simple: with my ORCID, ADS library, or Google Scholar account, this tool can automatically search for these papers and generate a brief summary in both English and Chinese that highlights the creative features and new results of these works. I also want to keep a record of the key figures for use on my website.
- Status: Under Testing
- Specification: `PROJECT2_SPEC.md`
- Folder: `project2`

## Project 3: Farming the RSS Feed of Major Journals in Astronomy

- Motivation: Just following the arXiv update might not be enough; it is also useful to keep an eye on the recent issues of the major journals in astronomy and cosmology (ApJ/ApJL/ApJS, MNRAS, A&A, Nature Astronomy, PASP, PASJ, and JCAP). The websites of these journals often offer RSS feeds for the most recent issues, allowing regular updates and a record of relevant papers.
- Status: Under Testing
- Specification: `PROJECT3_SPEC.md`
- Folder: `project3`

## Project 4: A Light-weight, Local-only, Tag-based Figure Management System

- Motivation: When we read papers, documents, or collect information from the internet, screenshots and saved figures are often the most condensed and informative way (at least to me, a more visual-inclined person) to memorize and internalize key information. For example, for publications, there usually are a few "money plots" or key figures that summarize the most critical idea or conclusion. However, it is not easy to organize a ton of screenshots. Even just rename them can be an annoying task. The idea here is to build an "interactive database" for all the figures I collected. This tool should help me "memorize" important figures with an informative name, tags, and other metadata. It should have a simple and clean GUI and is based on local storage only. 
- Status: Under planning
- Folder: `project4`
